# Payments and delivery in Nepal

Background for the assistant. Use the user's real payment methods, delivery partners, charges and times. Never state a delivery charge, delivery time, wallet fee or courier coverage the user has not given; ask instead.

## How buyers pay

| Method | What to know for launch copy | Source (read date) |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile banking and QR (Fonepay, NepalPay QR and bank apps) | QR is now the most widely used retail payment instrument in Nepal. In mid-March to mid-April 2026, QR payments were 54.64 million transactions worth Rs 153.47 billion; mobile banking was 74.46 million worth Rs 618.71 billion. A shop QR on the post, the parcel and the counter is normal. | Nepal Rastra Bank, Payment Systems Oversight Report FY 2024/25; NRB data reported by The Himalayan Times, 12 May 2026 (read 2026-11-20) |
| Digital wallets (eSewa, Khalti, IME Pay and others) | Very familiar names; buyers often ask "eSewa/Khalti ma tirna milcha?". Wallet payment links or merchant QR suit online orders. | NRB data via People's Review, 17 June 2026 (read 2026-11-20) |
| connectIPS and bank transfer | Common for higher-value and B2B orders, and for advance payments. | NRB payment systems indicators (read 2026-11-20) |
| Cash on delivery (COD) | Still the trust default for first orders from a new seller, particularly outside the Kathmandu Valley. Costs the seller: courier COD fees, cash collection delay, and refused parcels. | Market practice; confirm the user's own refusal rate |
| Cards | Debit card use is common; credit cards are much less common than in other markets. Do not build a launch around card-only checkout. | NRB indicators (read 2026-11-20) |

Copy rules:
- Name only the payment methods the user actually accepts.
- If the user wants fewer COD refusals, suggest a small advance or "pay by QR on delivery" only as an option for the user to decide; never present it as a requirement the user has not set.
- Never write "secure payment" or "100% safe" without the user's basis.

## Paying for ads from Nepal

- Facebook, Instagram, Google and TikTok ads are paid in foreign currency. Nepal Rastra Bank requires such payments to go through the banking channel, typically a bank-issued dollar card ("e-com card"); paying through informal channels such as hundi or relatives abroad is treated as illegal. Sources: Republica, 1 February 2020; ICT Frame, 11 February 2020; Global IME Bank e-com card page (read 2026-11-20).
- Since Shrawan 2082 (mid-2025), Nepal levies a 2% electronic service tax on targeted online advertising sold by foreign platforms to Nepali advertisers; the tax is payable by the non-resident platform. Source: Ekantipur, 14 August 2025 (read 2026-11-20).
- Dollar-card limits are set per bank and per NRB rules and change; tell the user to confirm their card's annual limit before planning a large ad budget. Many small businesses buy boosting through a registered agency that bills in rupees with VAT.

## Delivery

- Common options: the business's own riders, delivery companies and courier/logistics partners (examples Nepali sellers use include Pathao, Upaya, Nepal Can Move and others), bus or cargo dispatch for distant districts, and marketplace logistics for Daraz sellers. Names are examples, not recommendations; coverage and rates change.
- Inside-valley and outside-valley charges and times are usually different; copy should show both if the user gives both.
- "Free delivery" must state its real condition (minimum order, inside valley only, launch week only) exactly as the user sets it.
- Monsoon and festival peaks slow delivery. For orders taken in the week before Dashain or Tihar, promise only what the courier has confirmed to the user.

## Returns, exchange and warranty

- State the user's real terms: how many days, which items, who pays the return delivery, refund or exchange only, and how to request it.
- Warranty claims for electronics should name who honours the warranty (brand, authorized distributor or the seller). Never write "official warranty" unless the user confirms the seller is authorized.
- If the user has no return policy yet, say a launch page needs one and ask what it is; do not invent one.
