# Nepal launch calendar: seasons, festivals and buying windows

Nepal runs on the Bikram Sambat (BS) calendar; the Gregorian dates of festivals move by 10 to 20 days from year to year. The windows below are typical. **Always confirm exact dates** for the launch year from the Ministry of Home Affairs public-holiday list, the Nepal Panchang Nirnayak Samiti, or a calendar app the user trusts (for example Hamro Patro), and say which source you used. Never print a festival date from memory as fact.

Reference point (for checking your conversion, not for planning): in 2026 (2083 BS), Ghatasthapana fell on 11 October and Vijaya Dashami tika on 21 October; Tihar followed in early November. Sources: MoHA holiday list via Hindutone; Ekantipur, 6 October 2026 (read 2026-11-20).

## BS months and their usual Gregorian window

| BS month | Approx. Gregorian | Commercial notes |
|---|---|---|
| Baisakh | mid-April to mid-May | Nepali New Year (Baisakh 1): "Naya Barsha" offers, new-year resolutions, wedding season. |
| Jestha | mid-May to mid-June | Pre-monsoon heat; Buddha Jayanti often falls in April–May. School admissions continue. |
| Asar | mid-June to mid-July | Monsoon starts; **fiscal year ends at the end of Asar** (mid-July): government and corporate budgets are spent or lost, so B2B and institutional launches push hard here. Ropain (rice planting) on Asar 15. |
| Shrawan | mid-July to mid-August | New fiscal year, new budgets allocated; monsoon delivery delays; women's Shrawan month (green bangles, mehendi). |
| Bhadra | mid-August to mid-September | Teej (women's festival: red saree, jewellery, cosmetics, beauty services) and Janai Purnima often fall here or late Shrawan. Indra Jatra in Kathmandu around September. |
| Ashwin | mid-September to mid-October | **Dashain** usually begins (Ghatasthapana). Biggest buying season starts: clothes, gold, electronics, home appliances, mobile phones, motorbikes, travel, goat and meat, gift hampers. Remittance and Dashain allowance arrive. |
| Kartik | mid-October to mid-November | Vijaya Dashami tika, then **Tihar** (Laxmi Puja: gold, lights, sweets, home décor; Bhai Tika: gifts between siblings). Chhath in the Terai. Mha Puja and Nepal Sambat new year for Newar communities. Peak tourist season. |
| Mangsir | mid-November to mid-December | Peak **wedding season**: jewellery, banquets, photography, fashion, gifts, honeymoon travel. Post-festival lull in impulse buying for some categories. |
| Poush | mid-December to mid-January | Winter: warm clothes, heaters, blankets. Christmas and 1 January celebrated in cities. Tamu Lhosar (Gurung) around late December. |
| Magh | mid-January to mid-February | Maghe Sankranti (Magh 1): sweets, ghee, chaku, til laddu; Maghi for the Tharu community. Sonam Lhosar (Tamang) often here. Weddings resume. |
| Falgun | mid-February to mid-March | Gyalpo Lhosar (Sherpa) and Maha Shivaratri often fall here or early March. Wedding season continues. |
| Chaitra | mid-March to mid-April | Holi (Fagu Purnima) often in March; **SEE exams** for grade 10 students; end-of-BS-year sales ("Chaitra sale") before New Year. |

## Planning rules

1. **Do not launch during the main festival days themselves** (roughly Phulpati to Tika for Dashain, and Laxmi Puja to Bhai Tika for Tihar) unless the product is the festival purchase. Shops, couriers and offices close; staff travel home. Launch 2–4 weeks before, so buyers find the product while planning festival spending.
2. **Courier and stock cut-offs.** Ask the user for their courier's last-dispatch date before Dashain and Tihar; put that date into the plan as a hard deadline.
3. **Ad costs rise in festival weeks** because many advertisers compete for the same buyers. Plan a test budget in the quieter weeks before.
4. **Monsoon** (about June to September) slows delivery to hill districts; avoid promising fixed delivery times outside the valley.
5. **Wedding seasons** (Mangsir, Magh–Falgun, Baisakh) suit fashion, jewellery, events, photography, electronics and home goods.
6. **Academic calendar**: SEE results and +2/bachelor admissions (roughly June to August) suit education products, laptops, phones, coaching and consultancies.
7. **B2B**: launch to businesses and institutions before the fiscal-year end (Asar) or right after new budgets (Shrawan–Bhadra). Avoid the festival month for meetings.
8. **Diaspora gifting**: Nepalis abroad buy gifts for family before Dashain and Tihar; open orders and payment from abroad 3–4 weeks ahead if the user can deliver.
9. **Respect**: festival creative must use the festival correctly (tika, jamara, diyo, rangoli) and never mock rituals or a community. If unsure about a community's festival custom, ask the user rather than guess.
