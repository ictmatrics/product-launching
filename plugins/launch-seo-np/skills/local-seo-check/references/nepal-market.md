# Nepal market context for product launches

Background for the assistant. Figures carry a source and a read date. If a read date is more than 6 months old when you use a figure, say it may be out of date and point the user to the source. Never present these figures as the user's own market size; they describe the country, not the user's segment.

What this means for a launch, in plain words:
- Facebook and Messenger are the default storefront and help desk for most Nepali small businesses. TikTok, Instagram and YouTube matter for discovery, especially for buyers under 30. Viber and WhatsApp carry repeat orders, B2B follow-ups and family-group sharing.
- Almost half the population is still not on social media. Launches aimed at older buyers, farmers or rural districts still need offline routes: dealers and retailers, FM radio, local newspapers, haat bazaar stalls, cooperatives, word of mouth.
- Ad audience counts are estimates of accounts, not people. Never quote them as customers.

## Platform access risk

Platform access in Nepal has changed suddenly more than once: TikTok was blocked from November 2023 until August 2024, and on 4 September 2025 the government ordered 26 unregistered platforms, including Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and YouTube, blocked under the Directive on Regulating the Use of Social Media, 2080; the block was reversed within days amid mass protests. Sources: The Himalayan Times, 4 September 2025; TechCrunch, September 2025 (read 2026-11-20).

Launch rule that follows: never build a launch on one platform only. Every launch plan keeps at least one owned channel the business controls: a phone number, a Viber community or SMS list, an email list, a website or a marketplace store.

## Languages and scripts

- Nepali in Devanagari is the national language and reads as respectful and trustworthy.
- Romanized Nepali ("kati ho price?", "COD milcha?") is how most buyers type in comments and DMs. Mirror it in replies; do not use it on packaging, legal lines or price lists.
- English is expected for tech, education, premium and B2B products, and code-mixing is normal: "Dashain offer ma free delivery".
- In Madhesh and parts of Lumbini and Koshi, Maithili, Bhojpuri and Hindi are widely spoken; Newar (Nepal Bhasa) in the Kathmandu Valley; Tamang, Tharu, Limbu, Magar and other languages in their communities. Use them only when the user provides or approves the wording; never machine-guess a language the user did not ask for.
- Numbers: Nepali buyers read lakh and crore (1,00,000 = 1 lakh). Write prices as "Rs 1,499" or "रु. १,४९९"; keep the user's own format once chosen.
- Dates: many buyers think in Bikram Sambat (BS). For deadlines, give both: "Mangsir 15 (1 December)". Convert only with a reliable converter; if you cannot verify a conversion, give the date the user gave and ask.

## Geography that changes the plan

- **Kathmandu Valley** (Kathmandu, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur): densest buyers, same- or next-day delivery, most competition, highest ad costs per result in practice.
- **Other major cities**: Pokhara, Butwal–Bhairahawa, Chitwan (Bharatpur), Biratnagar, Itahari–Dharan, Birgunj, Hetauda, Nepalgunj, Dhangadhi, Janakpur. Delivery typically takes longer and costs more; outside-valley buyers lean harder on cash on delivery.
- **Hill and remote districts**: delivery can take days to weeks; monsoon landslides (roughly June to September) cut roads. Promise delivery times only from the user's actual courier terms.
- **Diaspora**: millions of Nepalis work or study abroad (Gulf, Malaysia, India, Japan, Korea, Australia, USA, UK). Many buy for family at home, especially before Dashain and Tihar. A "send a gift home" route can be a launch segment on its own.

## How Nepali buyers decide (patterns, not guarantees)

- **"Price?" in the comments.** Many buyers ask the price publicly. Hiding the price behind "inbox" loses buyers and annoys them; show the price in the post unless the user has a reason not to.
- **Trust before payment.** First-time buyers check: is there a physical shop or address, a phone number that answers, real reviews or comments, how long the page has existed, whether friends follow it. A new brand should lead with these.
- **Cash on delivery (COD) is the default for new sellers.** Digital wallets are rising fast (see `payments-logistics.md`), but a buyer who has never heard of the brand often wants to see the product before paying.
- **Returns and exchange.** "Size milena bhane?" ("what if the size doesn't fit?") is one of the most common objections. State the real exchange terms near the order button.
- **Group buying and referrals.** Families and offices buy together; "refer a friend" and bundle offers fit naturally.
- **Festival budgets.** Dashain allowance (a month's extra pay for many salaried workers) and remittance inflows before Dashain and Tihar make October–November the biggest consumer buying window. See `festival-calendar.md`.
- **Price sensitivity with status buying.** Buyers compare with Daraz, Indian imports and local shops; they also pay a premium for "original", gifting and visible brands. Position on value, not on "cheapest".

## Competitive reality check

Before a launch plan, list for the user (from their facts, never invented): who sells the same thing on Daraz, on Facebook pages, in local shops, and as Indian or Chinese imports; their visible price; how they deliver; what buyers complain about in their comments. If the user has not supplied this, ask for it or offer a checklist for them to fill in. Do not name competitors you have not been given or verified.
