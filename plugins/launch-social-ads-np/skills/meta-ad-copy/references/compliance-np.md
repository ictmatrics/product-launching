# Rules a Nepali launch should check

This file is background so the assistant can flag issues. It is not legal advice and does not decide whether anything is permitted. When you mention a rule, give its name and tell the user to confirm with the official text or a Nepali lawyer. If a read date below is more than 6 months old, say the rule may have changed.

## Registering to sell online

- **Electronic Commerce (E-Commerce) Act, 2081.** Certified on 3 Chaitra 2081 (16 March 2025) and in force from 15 April 2025. Anyone selling goods or services online in Nepal, including sellers who only use Facebook, TikTok or Instagram, must be listed on the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection e-commerce portal before trading. Existing sellers were given until mid-July 2025; unregistered sellers face fines (reported up to Rs 200,000) and other action. An Electronic Commerce Directive, 2082 implements the Act. Sources: Law Bhandari & Associates; The Kathmandu Post, 8 and 20 July 2025; Ratopati English (read 2026-11-20).
- The Act also expects the seller's identity and contact details, product details, price, delivery and return terms to be shown to buyers. A launch page or page "About" section should carry the seller's registered name, listing number if the user has one, address, phone and terms.
- Company or firm registration, PAN or VAT registration and the e-commerce listing are separate steps. Ask whether the user has each; do not assume.
- In January 2026 the government brought online business platforms under ICT minimum standards, with audit and certification by the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection. Source: The Kathmandu Post, 10 January 2026 (read 2026-11-20). Relevant if the user runs a platform, not if they only sell on one.

## Advertising

- **Advertisement (Regulation) Act, 2076 (2019)** and **Advertisement (Regulation) Rules, 2077**, amended in 2080 (2023). Apply across print, broadcast, online, social media and hoardings. Misleading advertisements are prohibited; an Advertisement Board oversees standards, and the Act lists restricted content, including advertisements that discriminate by religion, gender, caste, financial status or language, and use of trademarks or patents without approval. Pradhan & Associates' summary of the 2080 amendment notes that a person pictured in an advertisement must be a Nepali national; flag this when a user plans foreign models and tell them to confirm the current rule. Sources: Pioneer Law; Pradhan & Associates; Law Commission (read 2026-11-20).
- **Pre-approval of audio-visual ads.** The Board has stated that audio, visual and audio-visual advertisement material should be vetted by it under Section 15(b) of the Act before dissemination, and in 2026 ordered pre-approval of broadcast adverts. Source: B360 Nepal (read 2026-11-20). Tell the user to check whether their TV, radio or video ad needs Board approval.
- **Social media warnings.** In June 2026 the Board warned of action against misleading and baseless advertisements on social media, and has asked the Nepal Telecommunications Authority to shut down accounts posting unauthorized misleading ads. Sources: Radio Nepal, 18 June 2026; The Rising Nepal, 15 October 2026 (read 2026-11-20).
- **National Advertising Policy, 2083** (approved by the Cabinet on 2 July 2026). Brings social media, digital platforms, influencer marketing, sponsored content and AI-generated advertising under regulation. Clause 9.13: sponsored content and influencer marketing require mandatory self-declaration; AI-generated advertising, including deepfakes, will be regulated. Advertisements that disrespect national symbols, or discriminate by class, caste, religion, language, culture, community or gender, are not allowed. The policy is being implemented through law and rules; check the current status. Sources: The Kathmandu Post, 5 July 2026; Republica; B360 Nepal (read 2026-11-20).

Practical rules this plugin applies because of the above:
1. Every paid influencer or creator post is labelled as sponsored ("Sponsored", "Paid partnership", "विज्ञापन" or the platform's paid-partnership label).
2. No AI-generated face, voice or testimonial is presented as a real person. No deepfake of any real person, ever.
3. No unbacked superlatives ("No. 1 in Nepal", "best", "cheapest") or medical, financial or result claims without evidence.
4. No content that mocks or targets a caste, ethnicity, religion, region, language, gender or disability, even as a joke.
5. No use of the national flag, anthem or national figures for selling unless the user confirms it is permitted.

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Consumer Protection Act, 2075 (2018)** protects consumers against misleading information and unfair trade practices, and covers price and product information. Prices quoted should be the real price the buyer pays, with VAT and delivery stated clearly.
- **Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018)** protects personal information. Do not collect, publish or share buyers' phone numbers, addresses or chats beyond what the order needs; get consent before adding buyers to SMS, Viber or email lists.
- **Electronic Transactions Act, 2063** covers electronic records, digital signatures and some online offences.

## Sector rules to flag (ask the user, never guess)

- Medicines, supplements, Ayurvedic and herbal health products: the Department of Drug Administration (DDA) regulates claims and registration. No cure or treatment claims without the user's registration evidence.
- Food: the Department of Food Technology and Quality Control (DFTQC) licensing and labelling. No "organic", "chemical-free" or health claims without certification.
- Alcohol and tobacco: advertising is heavily restricted. Do not write promotional copy for them; tell the user to get legal advice.
- Education consultancies, foreign employment, finance, insurance, real estate and cooperatives have their own regulators and advertising rules; flag and ask.
- Products carrying the NS (Nepal Standard) mark or certificates: mention only with the certificate the user holds.

## Tax lines in price copy

Nepal's standard VAT rate is 13%. If the user is VAT-registered, ask whether their price includes VAT and write it the way they confirm ("Rs 1,130 incl. VAT"). Never compute or state a tax figure the user has not confirmed.
